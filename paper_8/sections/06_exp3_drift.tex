\section{Experiment 3: Does Drift Order the Outcome?}
\label{sec:exp3}
% ============================================================

CKA is computed after fine-tuning, on the model fine-tuning changed, so it cannot
replace the intervention as a decision rule.  It is worth testing anyway because
that is how it is used: analyses of fine-tuned models report a CKA drop as
evidence of forgetting (\S\ref{sec:intro}).  The question is whether such a drop
carries information about the outcome the intervention measures.  We test it in
three ways, from the most direct to the most aggregate.

\label{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}
\textbf{Counterexamples within one backbone.}  Within Moirai-Base the two
\emph{least} drifted cells are the only two that degrade (ETTh1 at CKA $0.755$
and $0.669$, $+22.8\%$ and $+39.5\%$), while its two most drifted improve the
most (ETTh2 at CKA $0.460$ and $0.396$, $-31.4\%$ and $-36.3\%$;
Table~\ref{tab:dissociation}).  On Moirai-Small/ETTh2, one of the seven
survivors, sweeping $n{\in}\{500,\ldots,10\mathrm{k}\}$ drives CKA down
monotonically from $0.949$ to $0.518$ while held-out forgetting \emph{falls}, from
$+5.2\%$ at 500 to $-18.5{\pm}2.8\%$ at 10k, with every seed improving on
zero-shot from $n{=}1$k on: more drift, better outcome
(Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep}, Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}).  Inside one encoder, unfreezing the top 3 of 6
layers and all 6 gives indistinguishable task outcomes at CKA $0.716$ and $0.465$
(10 seeds each; Appendix~\ref{app:layerunfreeze}).  Across mitigation strategies
on that cell, the three weight-anchoring arms span CKA $0.939$--$0.972$ and land
within $0.003$ MSE of one another (Appendix~\ref{app:full_tables}).  Each of these
contradicts ``lower CKA, more damage'' as a rule; none of them establishes the
reverse.

\textbf{Other backbones.}  None of the nine Chronos and TimesFM cells passes the
gate and their training protocols differ (Table~\ref{tab:trainers}), so we read
them beside Moirai rather than pooling them.  Four of the five Chronos-T5-Small
cells restructure to CKA $0.092$--$0.169$, at or below the $0.215{\pm}0.054$ of an
\emph{untrained} encoder of this architecture (Appendix~\ref{app:cka_calibration}),
yet their outcomes span $-41.4\%$ on ETTm2 to $+20.6\%$ on ETTh1, and ETTh1, the
least drifted, is the only one that degrades.  The four TimesFM-2.5 cells sit at
milder drift and all improve (Table~\ref{tab:crossbackbone}).

\textbf{Association across cells.}  Pooled over all 31 cells, CKA correlates with
$\denc$ ($\rho{=}{+}0.567$, clustered CI $[{+}0.239,{+}0.828]$), but that is mostly
the gap between backbones, which a practitioner knows before computing anything
(Figure~\ref{fig:screen}b).  Within Moirai, the one backbone with enough cells,
$\rho{=}{+}0.168$ (22 cells, 6 clusters, CI $[-0.469,{+}0.717]$; under F${=}$H,
\pending{$\rho$ and CI}).  The interval excludes a strong positive association but
not a moderate one.  This design would detect a true $\rho$ of $0.50$ with
$80\%$ power (Appendix~\ref{app:clustered}), so an association of $0.5$ or more
would probably have shown; a weaker one may exist, and we
claim only that CKA does not \emph{establish} an ordering.  Acting on CKA as the prescriptions do,
freezing where drift is largest, is worth $-6.97$~pp $[-10, +14]$ against always
adapting (Appendix~\ref{app:decision}), and $\ell_2$ weight drift and five further
geometric measures do no better (Appendices~\ref{app:crosscell},~\ref{app:driftmetrics}).

\textbf{Out-of-sample prediction.}  With the predictor ladder and the deciding
statistic fixed in advance, leave-one-cluster-out $R^2$ over the 15 clusters rises
from $+0.000$ to $+0.137$ on backbone identity and to $+0.201$ with horizon and
$\log n$, then \emph{falls} to $+0.176$ once CKA is added ($\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.025$;
no improvement in $69\%$ of cluster-bootstrap draws).  What is predictable about
$\denc$ here is predictable from the protocol; CKA adds nothing measurable to it
(Appendix~\ref{app:loco}).
